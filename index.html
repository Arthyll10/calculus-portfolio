Operations on Functions

View activity & reflection

Reflection

This was the first activity, and honestly it felt more like algebra than calculus. Adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing functions isn't hard on its own, but one dropped negative sign throws off the whole answer. I learned to slow down, simplify one step at a time, and check which x-values are actually allowed before calling it done.

The Chain Rule

View activity & reflection

Reflection

The chain rule confused me at first because everything seemed to happen at once. What helped was splitting the function into an outside layer and an inside layer. Once I could see the layers, the steps made sense: differentiate the outside, leave the inside alone, then multiply by the derivative of the inside. That last part is the one I kept forgetting.

Implicit, Exponential & Trig

View activity & reflection

Reflection

This activity mixed implicit, exponential and trig derivatives, so I had to figure out what kind of function I was looking at before doing anything. Implicit differentiation took the most getting used to, especially remembering the dy/dx every time I differentiated a y term. I learned to apply the rules first and only simplify at the very end.

Integral Drills

View activity & reflection

Reflection

Integration felt like doing derivatives in reverse, which sounds simple but took real practice. These drills helped me spot the forms I could integrate right away. I also got into the habit of writing + C every time, and differentiating my answer to make sure it gives back the original function.

Reflection

Trig integrals showed me that a problem that looks impossible can become manageable once it's rewritten. Choosing the right identity or substitution makes a huge difference. Next time I want to take a moment to plan my approach before diving in, instead of switching methods halfway through.
